# Art Deco Dementlieu
Tone layer for the campaign. **Art deco first, with Gatsby-style parties**, laid over Saidra's [[Dementlieu]].
The pitch: *the party never ends, because the moment it does, everyone has to admit they're broke, starving, and dead inside.*

## How Far to Push the Tech
Pick a dial and stick with it:
- **Light touch:** Gaslight, horse-drawn carriages, gramophones. The 1920s shows up only in fashion, music, and attitude.
- **Full Jazz Age (recommended):** Electric marquees, phonographs, cameras with flash powder, a few motorcars that only the ultra-rich own. Nobody knows where the electricity comes from. (Lamordian imports through the Mists?)
- Firearms: stay with D&D weapons and reflavor them. A hand crossbow is a "pocket piece," and a heavy crossbow is a "chopper."

## The Look: Art Deco Style Guide [Homebrew]
**The core idea:** Art deco is the style of perfect surfaces: geometry, polish, gold, the confidence of the machine age. Dementlieu is a city that is *all surface*. Deco is the face it wears over the rot. Every description should be beautiful, symmetrical, and gleaming, with one thing slightly wrong.

### Motifs
- **Sunbursts:** on doors, elevator panels, ballroom floors, and the backs of chairs. Saidra's personal emblem is a **sunburst with no sun at the center**, just a black circle.
- **Chevrons and zigzags:** banding on every facade, frame, and hem.
- **Stepped forms:** ziggurat towers, stepped mirrors, stepped fountains, and stepped cakes at banquets.
- **Stylized figures:** elongated, faceless women holding torches; gazelles and greyhounds mid-leap; angels with straight-edged wings.
- **Fans and shells:** fan-shaped windows, scalloped lamps, peacock fans in gold leaf.
- **Clocks:** huge deco clock faces everywhere in the city, with geometric numerals. Many of them have stopped at midnight.

### Materials
- **Rich:** black marble, gold leaf, chrome, brass, lacquer, ebony, ivory, mirror glass, mother-of-pearl, shagreen (sharkskin), etched crystal.
- **What it really is:** gilt paint over plaster, brass that leaves green on your fingers, paste "diamonds," veneer peeling off pine. Let a sharp-eyed PC spot the difference.

### Palette
- **Black and gold** for the rich: the default for the d'Honaire Estate and the Heights.
- **Silver, white, and mirror** for the Mother of Tears and the "respectable" middle.
- **Jade, cream, and coral** for theaters, hotels, and salons.
- **Red** is never worn. It belongs to the Red Death, and anyone in red at a party draws a horrified silence.

### Architecture
- **The skyline:** Stepped towers lit gold at night, spires like fountain pens, crowns of glass and chrome. From the bay the city looks like a jewelry box. Up close the gold leaf is peeling.
- **Facades:** Carved relief panels of idealized workers, muses, and allegories like "Prosperity," "Commerce," and "Elegance." Nobody remembers who the faces are modeled on, and some have been chiseled off.
- **Gargoyles:** On the [[DHonaireEstate|d'Honaire Estate]] they're restyled as sleek gilded angels with blank faces, streamlined and wingless, staring out over the bay.
- **Old vs. new:** The few old-world buildings left, like the [[ThreeOddGables|Three Odd Gables]], look like stains among the sleek towers. Deco is the city pretending it has no past.

### Interiors
- Lobbies of black marble with sunburst elevator doors and an attendant in white gloves.
- Ballrooms with sprung floors inlaid in geometric patterns, walls of mirrors, and chandeliers like frozen waterfalls.
- Townhouse salons with chrome chairs, lacquered screens, a white baby grand, and a cocktail cabinet. In half of them, the rooms behind the salon are bare floorboards.

### Masks
- Deco masks in lacquer, enamel, and feathers. Each one is a geometric "brand," and people talk about "the Silver Chevron" or "the Lacquered Crescent" instead of using names.
- Styles: a half-mask of hammered silver rays, a full porcelain face with a single painted tear, a black lacquer oval with gold slits, a mask of fanned feathers set in chrome.
- The finest masks are made by one maskmaker on the Avenue whose shop never seems to be open.

### Fashion
- **Women:** Beaded and fringed dresses, dropped waists, long ropes of pearls, cigarette holders, cloche hats, feathered headbands, finger-wave bobs, silver lamé, long gloves.
- **Men:** Tuxedos and tails, white dinner jackets, spats, slicked hair, cufflinks, gold cigarette cases. Masks worn with everything.
- Everything is borrowed, pawned, or mended.

### Light
- Neon-gold marquees and frosted-glass uplights against the grey Mist. The Mist glows with reflected light, but the street below stays dark.
- Light in Dementlieu always comes from *below* or *behind*: faces lit from under the chin, figures silhouetted in doorways. It flatters, and it hides.

### Posters & Lettering
- Tall, thin, geometric lettering on every sign and invitation.
- Travel posters in every lobby advertise **"Summer in Chateaufaux!"** with stylized hills and a too-perfect sunset. They're the only "proof" the countryside exists.
- Masquerade invitations are black card with a gold sunburst, and the guest's mask (never their name) printed in silver.

### Deco Horror [Homebrew]
Make the style itself unsettling:
- **Too perfect:** Symmetry so exact it feels wrong. Two identical guests, two identical rooms, a hallway that repeats.
- **Faceless figures:** The stylized statues have no features, and sometimes they've turned their heads since last time.
- **Mirrors:** Mirrored walls everywhere, which means you'd notice who has no reflection. Saidra stands where the mirrors are draped.
- **Frozen motion:** Leaping gazelles and dancers in relief, caught mid-movement. In the d'Honaire Estate, some of the dancers in the friezes look like guests who disappeared.
- **Clocks:** Every clock in the estate has stopped at midnight, and they all tick at once when the Red Death is near.

## The Sound
- Jazz bands at every party, playing a little too fast, as if they're scared to stop. [Homebrew] *If the music at the Masquerade ever stops, the Red Death comes.*
- Torch singers at the [[RedWidowTheater|Red Widow]].
- Phonographs in empty townhouses play to rooms where nobody is listening.

## The Gatsby Layer
Saidra **is** Gatsby. She invented herself, she throws parties to be seen, and she's chasing a past she can't get back.

| Gatsby | Dementlieu |
|---|---|
| Gatsby's parties | The weekly Masquerade. Everyone comes, and nobody knows the host. |
| Gatsby reinventing himself | Saidra the farm girl became "Duchess d'Honaire" |
| Old money vs. new money | The Council's "old" families sneer at climbers, and they're all frauds too |
| The green light on Daisy's dock | **A green lantern** across the bay (see Julien Gastonne below) |
| The Valley of Ashes | **La Cendrée**: ash flats between the city and [[Chateaufaux]] |
| Eyes of Dr. T.J. Eckleburg | **The Watching Mask**: a giant faded advertisement over La Cendrée |
| "Rich girls don't marry poor boys" | Saidra's whole tragedy |
| Gatsby dead in his pool, no one at the funeral | What happens to every guest who's unmasked |

### La Cendrée (Valley of Ashes) [Homebrew]
- A grey wasteland at the city's edge where the **dust of the Red Death's victims** blows and settles. The poor live here.
- Ash-sweepers collect the dust. They sell it by the sack, and nobody asks who's buying.
- **The Watching Mask:** an enormous painted billboard of a deco mask with hollow eyes, advertising a perfume company that went under decades ago. Locals swear the eyes move. Maybe the Dark Powers really are watching.

### Julien Gastonne, the Man Across the Bay [Homebrew NPC]
- A new-money millionaire who throws the second-best parties in town on his barrier-island mansion. Nobody knows where his money comes from, and he never talks about his past.
- Every night he stands on his dock looking at the d'Honaire Estate, where **a green lantern** burns.
- He's in love with Saidra, or with the girl she was. He may be the brother/Duke's soul given a new face, or just a man who came out of the Mists remembering a dance.
- He wants the PCs to arrange a "chance" meeting. It ends in tragedy either way: she can't let him see her, and he can't stop looking.

## Art Deco Landmarks [Homebrew]
Deco versions of the book's locations:
- **[[DHonaireEstate|d'Honaire Estate]]:** a stepped white-and-gold palace on the water. Its ballroom has a sunburst floor in black and gold marble, the mirrored walls are etched with chevrons, and a huge stopped clock with deco numerals hangs over the stair.
- **[[MotherOfTearsCathedral|Mother of Tears Cathedral]]:** a soaring deco cathedral of setback towers. The stained glass is geometric, and the statue of Ezra has stylized streaming tears inlaid in silver.
- **[[RedWidowTheater|Red Widow Theater]]:** a marquee of red bulbs. The spider on the roof is chrome and red enamel, with its legs spread like a sunburst. Inside is velvet, mirrors, and a cocktail bar.
- **[[ThreeOddGables|Three Odd Gables]]:** the one place untouched by deco. Three crooked, old-world houses squat among the sleek towers like a bad memory.
- **The Governor's Tower:** the tallest building in the city, crowned with a lit spire. Marcel Guignol's office at the top looks out over everything he doesn't control.
- **The Grand Lucine Hotel:** a lobby of brass elevators, palm fronds, and gold leaf where the climbers go to be seen. Half the guests haven't paid in months.

## The Council & the Veiled Seat [Homebrew]
- The Council meets in a black marble chamber under a domed skylight of gold glass.
- **The Veiled Seat:** Saidra never comes to Council meetings in daylight. Her chair holds a blank black mask on a stand, and her voice comes out of it. The others assume it's a trick of sorcery. Nobody has ever checked whether anyone is behind it.

## The Party Circuit [Homebrew]
In Dementlieu a party isn't entertainment. It's survival. Being seen at the right party is how you prove you're still somebody. Not being invited means you're already finished.

### What an Opulent Party Looks Like
- **Champagne towers:** pyramids of coupe glasses taller than a man, refilled all night. The bottles are often watered down or refilled with cheap wine, and nobody says so.
- **Spectacle:** an orchestra on a floating stage, acrobats on silk ribbons, a live leopard on a jeweled leash, fireworks over the bay at the stroke of eleven (never midnight).
- **Excess:** oysters on mountains of ice, towers of petits fours, a fountain that pours perfume. Hosts bankrupt themselves for one night and spend the next year eating bread behind closed curtains.
- **Favors:** guests leave with gifts like silk scarves, gold cigarette cases, or a pearl in every napkin. Some of the pearls are paste.
- **The arrival:** everyone is announced at the top of a grand staircase by mask, not name. *"The Silver Chevron! The Lady of the Lacquered Crescent!"* A late or shabby arrival is talked about for weeks.
- **The dance:** the Charleston for the young and reckless, the waltz for the old guard. Being caught not knowing the steps is a small social death.
- **The midnight rule:** no party in Dementlieu goes past midnight. The bands stop at a quarter to twelve, and the guests rush out, leaving glasses, shoes, and the occasional dropped mask behind. (Except Saidra's. Hers is the only party that keeps going, and no one leaves it early.)

### The Season [Homebrew]
A calendar of events the party can scheme to get into:
- **The Opening Night at the Opéra Lucine:** the season's first big gathering. Box seats are bought, borrowed, or fought over.
- **The Regatta:** gilded gondolas race down the Grand Canal, followed by a garden party on the barrier islands.
- **The White Ball:** everyone wears white, and the first spot of a stain, wine or blood, is a scandal.
- **Julien Gastonne's Fridays:** the open house across the bay. Anyone can come, which is exactly why the old guard won't be seen there and secretly envies everyone who goes.
- **The Masquerade:** every week at the [[DHonaireEstate|d'Honaire Estate]]. The summit everyone is climbing toward.

### Where the Glittering Set Goes [Homebrew]
- **The Gilded Cage:** a supper club where the singers perform inside huge golden birdcages hung over the tables.
- **The Blue Gardenia:** a jazz cellar where the band never stops and the regulars never seem to leave.
- **The Grand Lucine Hotel rooftop:** cocktails under the stars, with the whole city glittering below. The Mist hides how small it is.
- **Madame Pernault's salon:** Thursday afternoons. Poetry, gossip, and the unofficial list of who might be invited to the Masquerade.

### Style Notes for the Table
- Describe the light: candlelight caught in crystal, gold reflected in black marble, sequins flashing.
- Describe the sound: laughter a little too loud, glass clinking, a trumpet over everything.
- Then describe one crack: a frayed cuff, a guest pocketing canapés, a host checking a pawn ticket, a servant's grey hand.

## Slang to Sprinkle In
- **"Gilded"**: rich, or pretending well. "She's gilded."
- **"Tarnished"**: exposed as poor. "Poor sap got tarnished."
- **"Dusted"**: killed by the Red Death.
- **"Bare-faced"**: unmasked; a terrible insult.
- **"Taking the long walk"**: heading for Chateaufaux, a euphemism for suicide or madness.
- **"Midnight money"**: cash that disappears when the illusion breaks.
- **"Going green"**: pining for something you can't have (from Julien's lantern).
- **"The bee's knees," "the cat's pajamas," "applesauce!"**: the classics, used a little too eagerly.
