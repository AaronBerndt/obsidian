# Monuments of the Gods

Ten monuments seeded across the world, tied to the [[Sea King]] and the wider pantheon
(Yeenoghu, the God of War, Quarum/Corum, Tidus, Miska/Queen of Chaos, the Seven Creators).
Each has a hook the party can pull on.

---

## Sea King & the Ocean Powers

### 1. The Drowned Colossus
A Sea King statue three times the height of the [[episode6|Skull Island]] monument, but sunk
to the waist in a shallow lagoon — as if the god waded ashore and was frozen mid-stride. Coral
has grown only on the *right* half; the left is unnaturally clean, scoured by a current that
runs the wrong way against the tide.
- **Hook:** At low tide a bronze door opens in the sole of the god's foot. Inside, the last
  intact reliquary of the [[episode6|Circle of Seafoam]] witches — and a warning about what the
  barrier over Skull Island really holds back.
- **Secret:** The clean half is where a Vaati (Deva) once leaned to carve the first stanza of
  the Staff of Seven's binding song. Touching it lets you *hear* the [[episode6|Seven Creators]].

### 2. The Tide-Choir
A ring of thirty-three basalt pillars standing in open water, each carved as a robed figure with
its mouth open. When the tide floods between them the whole ring *sings* — a chord that sailors
say is the Sea King counting the drowned. Ships that answer the chord back (a specific returning
note) are granted safe passage; ships that stay silent are hounded by storms.
- **Hook:** One pillar has gone silent this year. Something has plugged its throat from within.
- **Secret:** The mute pillar is a prison, not an instrument. A siren-queen bound by the witches
  sits inside, and the failing note is her working her way free.

### 3. The Anchor of Kings
A ship's anchor the size of a keep, half-buried on a headland far inland — proof, the fisherfolk
say, that the sea once reached this high before the Sea King withdrew it as punishment. Pilgrims
climb it to tie prayer-knots in its chains; the whole thing groans and shifts with the distant tide
even miles from any water.
- **Hook:** The tide it answers is not this world's. On nights it groans loudest, water seeps
  *upward* out of the ground around it — brine that shouldn't exist here.
- **Secret:** It marks a drowned Void Rift. The Sea King didn't withdraw the sea; he *capped*
  something with it, and the cap is thinning. (Optional tie to [[Tef]]'s "raise the city" arc —
  what if Liquid's city sits in the old seabed this anchor once held down?)

## The Death God & the God of War

### 4. The Obelisk of Ash (Yeenoghu)
The great obsidian obelisk in [[characters|The Scar]] — the one the gnoll death-priests guard.
Up close it isn't carved; the surface is *smooth as poured glass*, and the "carvings" are
figures suspended **inside** the black stone, mid-scream. The embalmed God of Death is rumored
to lie within.
- **Hook:** The figures in the glass have started facing a new direction — all now turned toward
  Dresk. The priests are terrified and won't say why.
- **Secret:** The obelisk is a lid on Yeenoghu's grave, not a tomb around his body. Every gnoll
  born is a drop of him leaking out. If the [[Tef|Vault of Vaneeroth]] *is* the gravesite, this
  is its true door.

### 5. The Sundered Blade
Where the God of War is said to have struck Yeenoghu down, a single sword blade — a hundred feet
of pitted iron — stands driven into the black sand to the hilt-guard. No two people agree on its
color; it looks freshly forged to warriors and rusted to ruin to the fearful. Blood (the God of
War's, they say) still beads on it at dawn and never falls.
- **Hook:** A drop of that dawn-blood will heal any wound once — but the wound it heals will
  reopen on whoever the drinker next kills.
- **Secret:** The blade is the boundary stone between the two gods' domains. Pull it and you
  don't get a weapon — you get the God of War's *attention*, and Yeenoghu's grave loses a nail.

### 6. The Gallows-Tree of the Twins
A petrified tree on the edge of The Scar hung with two iron effigies — a black-furred gnoll and
a white one. Ygara's tribe leaves offerings here for the twins Ygram and Gamal, one honored, one
cast out. The white effigy is always warm to the touch.
- **Hook:** A personal monument for [[characters|Ygara]] — she can learn here whether Gamal
  (her stolen albino son) lived, and that the death-priests kept him *because* an albino is
  seen as Yeenoghu made flesh.
- **Secret:** Gamal is alive and being raised as a vessel for the leaking god.

## Conquest, Rebirth & Chaos

### 7. The Spear-Field of Quarum
Ten thousand spears planted point-up across a hillside, each marking a Covenant zealot who died
"of worth." The [[Tef|Covenant of the Sword]] adds one for every warrior they judge worthy;
unworthy dead get nothing. At the crown stands an empty pedestal shaped to hold a single spear.
- **Hook:** The empty pedestal is sized for the **Spear of Kigeth**. The Covenant is hunting
  [[Tef]] not just for the key — they mean to plant *her* here.
- **Secret:** Whoever mounts the true spear on the pedestal is acclaimed Quarum's living
  champion by every zealot in earshot. Id'jakun would kill for this.

### 8. The Silverflame Font (Tidus)
A roadside shrine in the Dresk region: a basin of water that burns with cold, silvery-blue fire
that never consumes it. The Tidus faithful say a soul dipped here is "given back its next life
early." It's where the executed Tidus cleric was meant to be reborn — and the flame has guttered
low since the day he died on [[Tef]]'s spear.
- **Hook:** Relighting the font (a quest in itself) would let the murdered cleric's rebirth
  finally complete — potentially as an ally, a witness, or an accuser against Tef.
- **Secret:** Tef's fire and this flame are the same color when no one is looking. The font
  reacts to her. (Resolve or retire the Tidus-Tef thread here.)

### 9. The Web-Cathedral (Miska / Queen of Chaos)
Not built but *spun*: a vast structure of petrified spider-silk in the deep wilds, its "walls"
strung between standing stones, its "windows" cocoons. Demons of the [[episode6|Bez'no'rak]]
still pilgrimage here to the spot where Miska the Wolf Spider's blood first touched the earth.
Everything inside casts eight shadows.
- **Hook:** At the center hangs a cocoon that has been "gestating" since before the Rod of Seven
  was shattered. Something in it dreams, and its dreams leak corruption like the kind that ruined
  Skull Island.
- **Secret:** It's a splinter of the Queen of Chaos herself, seeded to hatch when enough Rod
  pieces are reunited — a doomsday clock the party may be *winding* every time they collect one.

## The Makers

### 10. The Sevenfold Cenotaph (The Vaati / Seven Creators)
Seven identical thrones of white stone arranged in a circle around an empty eighth space, on a
mountaintop above the clouds. Each throne is worn as if long-sat-in, though the Vaati poured
themselves into the Staff and Rod of Seven ages ago and never returned. Whisper a question here
and, some say, seven voices answer at once — never agreeing.
- **Hook:** A living wielder of a Rod/Staff piece (Lex-touched, per [[episode6]]) who sits the
  empty eighth space can *petition* the Seven — but risks the melding accelerating, their
  identity dissolving into the makers.
- **Secret:** The empty throne was never for a Vaati. It was left for whoever would one day
  *reassemble* the Rod of Seven — the seat of the next Creator, or the next tyrant.
